# Quality Review Checklist

Use this before approving or publishing an article.

## Quick Verdict

Answer these first:

- Does the article satisfy the search intent?
- Would the target reader trust this article?
- Does the article fit the site's business?
- Is it meaningfully better than a generic AI article?
- Is there anything that could create legal, financial, medical, or credibility risk?

If any answer is weak, request changes before approval.

## Article Checks

Title:

- Specific and natural.
- Matches the article content.
- Not clickbait.
- Not overloaded with keywords.

Slug:

- Short.
- Lowercase.
- Descriptive.
- No unnecessary stop words.

Meta description:

- Clear value proposition.
- Around 140-160 characters when possible.
- No fake urgency.

Introduction:

- Gets to the point quickly.
- Names the reader problem.
- Does not use generic filler.

Body:

- Uses H2/H3 headings logically.
- Gives specific steps, examples, criteria, or frameworks.
- Avoids repetition.
- Avoids unsupported claims.
- Explains caveats.
- Includes internal-link opportunities.

Conclusion:

- Summarizes the useful takeaway.
- Gives a next step.
- Does not introduce a new unrelated topic.

Image prompt:

- Detailed enough for manual generation.
- Matches the article.
- Includes aspect ratio.
- Includes what to avoid.

## SEO Checks

- Primary keyword appears naturally.
- Related terms are covered without stuffing.
- Search intent is clear.
- FAQ included only if it helps.
- Internal links are suggested or included.
- External claims are source-backed if factual/current.

## Risk Checks

For finance/business articles:

- No guaranteed income, returns, or outcomes.
- No personalized financial advice.
- Caveats included when needed.
- Current data is not invented.
- If prices, rates, laws, or platform rules are mentioned, verify them first.

For health/legal topics:

- Prefer avoiding unless the site has authority.
- Include strong caveats.
- Do not provide personalized professional advice.

## Final Decision

Use one of:

- Approve: article is ready to publish.
- Request changes: article is useful but needs edits.
- Reject: article is off-strategy, unsafe, or too weak to repair.

When requesting changes, give precise instructions:

```text
Decision: Request changes

Main issue:

Required edits:
1.
2.
3.

Optional improvements:
1.
2.
```
